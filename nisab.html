Nisab Today (Late October 2026)

Nisab is the minimum amount of zakatable wealth. It is defined by weight: 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver (7.5 and 52.5 tola). These are the weights used in the Hanafi tradition and across South Asia. Many other institutions use 85 g of gold and 595 g of silver, so both are shown below.

Gold or silver: which nisab should I use?

Silver and gold were close in value in the Prophetic era. Today the gold nisab is about ten times higher than the silver nisab. Scholars and charities differ. Many recommend the silver nisab for cash and savings because it is the cautious option and brings more people into giving; others use gold. A person whose wealth is mostly gold normally checks against gold. If you are unsure, ask a scholar you trust, and choose the lower (silver) figure if you want to be on the safe side.

The Hawl: one lunar year

After nisab, the second condition is Hawl: your wealth must be held for one full lunar year (about 354 days). Scholars differ on what happens if your wealth drops below nisab during that year:

  • Hanafi school: a dip below nisab in the middle of the year does not restart the year, as long as you had nisab at the beginning of the year and still have it on the due date.
  • Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali schools: if wealth falls below nisab during the year, the Hawl count restarts from the date it reaches nisab again.

Whichever view you follow, Zakat is 2.5% of your zakatable wealth on your Zakat due date, after allowed deductions. Most people choose one fixed due date and calculate everything on that date.

Qualifying Assets for Zakat

Liquid Capital & Cash

Zakat is mandatory on all physical cash, bank deposits, and even money lent to others. This includes funds saved for long-term goals like Hajj or property.

Precious Metals

Our terminal tracks 24k, 22k, and 18k spot rates. Zakat is due on the pure weight of gold and silver in your possession, including bars and jewelry.

Digital & Equity Assets

Stocks, Sukuk, and Cryptocurrencies (Bitcoin, Stablecoins) must be valued at current market price for an accurate Nisab comparison.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Nisab should I follow: Gold or Silver?

Many contemporary scholars recommend the Silver Nisab as it ensures a lower threshold, benefiting more recipients of Zakat. However, both are valid Shariah standards.

Are retirement funds (401k/Pension) Zakatable?

Scholars differ. Many say the portion you can access is zakatable, while funds you cannot touch until retirement are treated more leniently by some. See the knowledge hub for the main views.

Can I subtract business debts?

Debts that are due now, such as supplier invoices and unpaid salaries, are generally deducted by scholars before comparing with nisab. Long-term loans such as a mortgage are treated differently by different schools, so ask a scholar if this applies to you.

What if my savings dip below nisab during the year?

Under the Hanafi view the year is not restarted if you had nisab at the start and have it on your due date. Under the Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali views the year restarts when you are back above nisab.

What about volatile Crypto assets?

Volatile assets should be valued at their market price on your 'Zakat Anniversary' date, regardless of previous highs or lows during the year.
